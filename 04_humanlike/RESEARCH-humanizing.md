# Give Meredith goals, not a script

Meredith reads as AI because she behaves too regularly, not because her words are wrong. The research tells that measurably separate humans from models are behavioral. Models answer every turn completely, mirror their partner, reuse one move pattern and recycle the same phrases across runs. Readers who use LLMs a lot also spot AI vocabulary and polish. Typos and fillers do not move them. Real disputes are messy. Opposition comes fast, the complainer repeats the same grievance, heat builds in steps, concessions are grudging, and the argument usually ends unresolved. The fix is to give her a private goal and a choice of moves, let turn length vary with the moment, and move policing out of the prompt into a checker script. Several of the specific tells you named (name use, "X, not Y", aphorisms) are practitioner claims, not measured findings, so test each change on its own before trusting it.

## Short snippets pass; long stretches expose the pattern

The strongest study of simulated conversation compared 200 Switchboard phone calls with model-generated ones. **Claude turns averaged 78-84 words against 14 for humans.** Models converged on their partner's ideas and syntax 1.5-2.5x more than humans did. **77% of one Claude setup's turns began with "oh".** Readers judged short snippets about as human as real ones (38% vs 44%), but **long excerpts only 29% vs 64%** ([Mayor et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12401190/)). Meredith's roughly 24-word average sits closer to human than that, but a blind reader of whole sessions sees the repetition that a single turn hides. RLHF cuts output diversity even across different inputs ([Kirk et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06452v2)), and different models converge on near-identical answers ([Artificial Hivemind](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121421)). That explains why the same stock lines recur across sessions. Sampling temperature will not fix a trained preference.

## The accurate tells are behavioral

In a pre-registered Turing test, **the same model was judged human 73% of the time with a persona prompt and 36% without one**. The judges' most accurate reasons were about how questions were handled, such as "always asks question back", not about typos. Dodging questions read as human ([Jones & Bergen](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23674)). Experienced LLM users misclassified only 1 of 300 AI articles, even after humanizer tools. They keyed on AI vocabulary and formality ([Russell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15654)). Meredith's fixed three-part reply (refuse the diversion, cite procedure, repeat the demand) is exactly this kind of regularity. Vocatives are a likely second tell. Film dialogue uses names more than real talk does ([Formentelli 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262574905_Vocatives_galore_in_audiovisual_dialogue_Evidence_from_a_corpus_of_American_and_British_films)), and in a two-person meeting the addressee is never in doubt. No study measures vocative rates in LLM output, so this is an inference. "X, not Y" and triplets appear in Wikipedia's AI-writing guide, where the tell is recurrence, not single use ([Beutler Ink summary](https://www.beutlerink.com/blog/how-to-spot-ai-writing)). Evidence there is practitioner-level.

## Real hostile parents repeat demands but not shapes

Repeating the demand is human, so keep it. Complainers "overwhelmingly" keep the original line of complaint after a denial ([Dersley & Wootton 2001](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/627/1/woottonaj1.pdf)). Parents in adversarial school meetings repeat the same grievance in near-identical words and press procedure: "Is there a written policy on that?" ([Bacon & Causton-Theoharis 2013](https://eclass-b.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PPP211/%CE%86%CF%81%CE%B8%CF%81%CE%B1%20%CE%B3%CE%B9%CE%B1%20%CF%84%CE%B7%CE%BD%20%CE%B5%CE%BE%CE%AD%CF%84%CE%B1%CF%83%CE%B7%20%CF%84%CE%BF%CF%85%20%CE%BC%CE%B1%CE%B8%CE%AE%CE%BC%CE%B1%CF%84%CE%BF%CF%82%20%CE%95%CE%B9%CE%B4%CE%B9%CE%BA%CE%AE%20%CE%91%CE%B3%CF%89%CE%B3%CE%AE/10_Bacon_Causton-Theoharis.pdf)). What is not human is the identical three-move package every turn. Real disputants vary the move. They build opposition from the opponent's own words, answer the literal question and then reject its frame, and escalate from the specific act to the general ([Goodwin 2006](https://colab.anthro.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/119/2022/09/2006c.-Retrospective-and-Prospective-Orientation-in-the-Construction-of-Argumentative-Moves.pdf)). Heat rises in steps. Complaints turn to disposition ("you always...") and to how the other side is arguing, and the peak comes after a refused request ([Dersley & Wootton](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/627/1/woottonaj1.pdf)). Concessions in disputes come late and hedged ([Kotthoff 1993](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-in-society/article/disagreement-and-concession-in-disputes-on-the-context-sensitivity-of-preference-structures/083137A4286CF71D8579852C9BB08BFF), abstract only). Using the teacher's words against them is opposition, which is different from the agreeable mirroring that models overdo.

## Prompt mechanics that backfire

Sample phrases in a prompt get used verbatim and repeated ([OpenAI GPT-4.1 guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt4-1_prompting_guide)). Demos from the same context as the live scene get copied most and perform worst, while diverse demos do best ([arXiv 2402.09954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09954)). Anthropic advises positive targets with reasons and 3-5 varied examples ([Claude best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)). "Don't mention X" can raise X's probability, but that rebound was small in the most modern model tested ([Mann et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12381v1)). Surface humanizing fails. Models misuse discourse markers ([Mayor et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12401190/)), and a persona written as a trait list tends toward caricature ([CoMPosT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.11501)).

| # | Change (testable) | Evidence and strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Replace the fixed reply template with a private state per turn (what she wants, fears and knows) and a menu of moves. Each turn picks one or two moves, sometimes a bare "No." or a non-answer. Test: share of turns that match the 3-move shape, under 25%. | Jones & Bergen, Goodwin. Moderate (behavioral tells measured; this ablation untested). | She can lose pressure or drift off topic. Keep the demand recurring. |
| 2 | Tie length to the moment: allow 2-6 word turns, keep the 3-sentence cap, and look for a wide spread, not a set mean. | Mayor (human mean 14, strong); [arXiv 2505.17390](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.17390). Strong. | A rule like "alternate short and long" makes a new rhythm. |
| 3 | Delete every quoted signature line from the rules. Describe behaviors. Any examples come from situations other than this meeting. | OpenAI guide, 2402.09954. Moderate. | Voice may go generic. Fix by describing her register, not by adding lines. |
| 4 | Use the teacher's name at most once per session, and only at a peak or to take the floor. | Formentelli (film over-uses names). Weak to moderate. | Low. |
| 5 | Keep banned constructions ("X, not Y", aphorisms, triplets, stock lines) out of the prompt. A checker script flags them, plus cross-session repeated openers and n-grams, and sends the turn back for regeneration. | Kirk, Hivemind (repetition, strong); the constructions themselves practitioner-level (weak). | Swapping one stock phrase for another. Compare n-grams against earlier sessions. |
| 6 | Make the record-keeping rare and implicit: one "I'd like that in writing" at most, and no reading numbers back. | Lawyer habit was an inference, unmeasured; procedural pressing supported (Bacon). Weak. | She can lose the lawyer edge. |
| 7 | Let the arc vary: heat rises after refusals, one grudging micro-concession walled off from the accusation, complaints about how the teacher is running the meeting, and an unresolved ending. | Dersley & Wootton (3 sequences), Kotthoff. Moderate. | Overacting, or playing every trait every turn. |
| 8 | Rewrite the rules as prose in her register, not headed bullets. | Anthropic (shown for markdown only). Weak. | Untested for register. |

**Avoid these:** injected typos, "um" or fillers, a long banned-phrase list inside the generator, catchphrases, full anger from turn 1, and agreeable mirroring of the teacher's register.

## Conclusion

The consistency that made Meredith reliable is what gives her away. The research points to a split: let the generator vary, and let a checker script outside the model hold the constraints. Perplexity is itself an LLM, and no source here tests whether its cues match a human reader's. Judge full sessions of 400+ words, change one rule per round, and count template matches and repeated n-grams. Do not trust a reworded prompt that only feels better.
